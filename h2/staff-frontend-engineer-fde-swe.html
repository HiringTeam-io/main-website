The Role

The interface is the product.

Our computer vision is strong. The interface where a person reviews its output is the product: an estimator opens a dense drawing, our model drops thousands of detected objects on it, and they pan, zoom, select and correct until the numbers are right. That canvas is what you'd own, and no one has built a good version of it yet.

This is a hands-on Staff role reporting to the VP of Engineering. You'd set frontend architecture and standards on an early codebase and be the most senior frontend voice in the room.

What You'll Own

1

The interactive canvas. Rendering, hit-testing, selection, editing and annotation over documents with thousands of live objects.

2

Frontend architecture. State model, component boundaries, type safety, testing, and the conventions the next ten engineers inherit.

3

Performance as a product requirement. Frame budget, memory, document load. "It gets slow on big drawings" loses the deal.

4

The AI review loop. The interaction patterns that let a user trust, correct and commit what the model produced.

5

Prototyping against real estimators. Form a hypothesis, get it in front of users in days, kill it fast when it's wrong.

6

The quality bar. Code review, documentation and technical judgment across a frontend team about to grow quickly.

What We're Looking For

Required & preferred experience.

● Required

  • 6+ years building software professionally, at least 5 on production frontend. Eight or more sits comfortably here.
  • Deep TypeScript, with React and Next.js in production — shipped and maintained, not weekend projects.
  • Real canvas or graphics work: tldraw, Konva, Fabric.js, PixiJS, Three.js, WebGL, D3, or your own renderer. The strongest signal for this role.
  • A complex feature owned end to end — from ambiguity, through production, through whatever broke afterward.
  • Performance instincts you can defend: virtualization, memoization, render batching, memory profiling — with reasons behind the choices.
  • Resilience to breaking API changes: runtime validation with Zod, generated types from OpenAPI or GraphQL, UI decoupled from raw API shapes.
  • Currently hands-on, and strong in writing. An IC role that stays one; we're across three countries, so decisions live in documents.

● Also Valuable

  • Document-heavy or annotation products: PDF viewers, design and diagramming tools, whiteboards, mapping, CAD/BIM.
  • B2B products with dense expert workflows.
  • Construction tech, CPQ/ERP or geospatial exposure.
  • Comfort working directly with customers.
